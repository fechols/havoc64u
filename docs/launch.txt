H.A.V.O.C. v0.1.2 development build - Heavy Assault Vessel: Orbital Combat

Requires a C64 Ultimate at 64 MHz with 16 MiB of REU memory and a joystick
connected to port 2. The supplied configuration enables the four built-in SIDs
and Ultimate Audio for digital music throughout the game, with true stereo
on the title, attract and victory screens. No extra sound chips are needed.

Launch
1. Copy havoc.cfg, havoc.reu, and havoc.prg to the Ultimate's storage.
2. Import havoc.cfg.
3. Load havoc.reu into the REU at address zero.
4. Run havoc.prg and press fire to launch.

Normal mode starts with 3 lives. On the title, press S to select Story mode
with 99 lives, then press fire to launch. Press S again to return to Normal.
This choice persists through retries and attract mode until you change it or
restart the program. S has no effect outside the title. Each lost life restarts
the current stage; fire after Game Over retries with 99 or 3 lives as selected.

Keep these three files from this package together. Import the configuration
again after using the Ultimate's SID player, which can change sound routing.
Re-import the current havoc.cfg to raise both digital music outputs to +6 dB;
the SID effects mixer gains remain at 0 dB. SID panning remains at Left 1 /
Right 1 for gentle headphone separation.
If startup reports a mismatch or invalid REU data, reload this package's REU
at zero before running its PRG again.

Upgrading from v0.1.1 or an earlier download? Replace all three runtime files
and re-import havoc.cfg to enable the 16 MiB REU and stereo music.

Turn your joystick's hardware autofire off. The game supplies its own autofire;
controller-generated fire pulses can accidentally spend your bombs.

Hammond Rocker plays continuously on the title and throughout the attract tour.
The final gold-medal reveal starts Galactic Dash. Release fire, then press it
to return to the title with Galactic Dash still playing.
Secret preview: CTRL + C= (Commodore) + DEL toggles the two digital songs on
the title or in attract mode, even before winning. Hold CTRL and C= and tap
DEL again to switch back. Each song starts from the beginning; the selection
stays until you switch again, complete the campaign, or reload the game.
All four SIDs are dedicated to layered effects with resonant filter sweeps.
Each effect uses one SID or a stereo pair, leaving chips free for overlapping
sounds. Player lasers and the tonal body of hull impacts are one octave higher
than the previous development build; impacts retain their soft, heavy attack.
Most other cues have a smaller pitch lift, while bombs and the largest
explosions keep their deep foundation. Effects still update at 50 Hz.
Barrel rolls have a stronger thruster sweep. Laser contact with
indestructible objects also makes a sound. Bombs use one stereo pair, leaving
another left and right chip for ship explosions and other effects; repeating
a bomb restarts its existing pair.
Shots and impacts choose a side from their screen position when triggered.
Only a narrow band around the center uses a stereo pair; existing tails keep
their original side. Bombs, boss explosions and player death remain stereo.
Starting a game selects its digital stage music while SID combat effects
continue. The first seven stages have original tracks: Lowland Approach,
Crystal Cave, Iron Canyon, Canyon Gate, Ashen Wastes, Foundry Belt, and Fortress Run.
Later stages currently use a temporary mono Galactic Dash recording.
Gameplay music uses 16 kHz, 3-bit mono with a 10 ms delay between outputs;
the title and victory recordings retain true stereo. Track changes fade the
outgoing music for about one second before the next song starts; the songs
do not overlap.
