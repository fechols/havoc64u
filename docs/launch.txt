H.A.V.O.C. v0.1.2 - Heavy Assault Vessel: Orbital Combat

Requires a C64 Ultimate at 64 MHz with 16 MiB of REU memory and a joystick
connected to port 2. The supplied configuration enables the four built-in SIDs
and Ultimate Audio for digital music throughout the game, with true stereo
on the title, attract and victory screens. No extra sound chips are needed.

Launch
1. Copy havoc.cfg, havoc.reu, and havoc.prg to the Ultimate's storage.
2. Import havoc.cfg.
3. Load havoc.reu into the REU at address zero.
4. Run havoc.prg and press fire to launch.

Keep these three files from this package together. Import the configuration
again after using the Ultimate's SID player, which can change sound routing.
Re-import the current havoc.cfg to raise both digital music outputs to +6 dB;
the SID effects mixer gains remain at 0 dB.
If startup reports a mismatch or invalid REU data, reload this package's REU
at zero before running its PRG again.

Upgrading from v0.1.1 or an earlier download? Replace all three runtime files
and re-import havoc.cfg to enable the 16 MiB REU and stereo music.

Turn your joystick's hardware autofire off. The game supplies its own autofire;
controller-generated fire pulses can accidentally spend your bombs.

Hammond Rocker plays continuously on the title and throughout the attract tour.
The final gold-medal reveal starts Galactic Dash. Release fire, then press it
to return to the title with Galactic Dash still playing.
Secret preview: CTRL + C= (Commodore) + DEL toggles the two digital songs on
the title or in attract mode, even before winning. Hold CTRL and C= and tap
DEL again to switch back. Each song starts from the beginning; the selection
stays until you switch again, complete the campaign, or reload the game.
Starting a game selects its digital stage music while SID combat effects
continue. The first six stages have original tracks: Lowland Approach,
Crystal Cave, Iron Canyon, Canyon Gate, Ashen Wastes, and Foundry Belt.
Later stages currently use a temporary mono Galactic Dash recording.
Gameplay music uses 16 kHz, 3-bit mono with a 10 ms delay between outputs;
the title and victory recordings retain true stereo.
