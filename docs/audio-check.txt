H.A.V.O.C. - Ultimate audio and combat frame-rate comparison

For this comparison, copy the matching new PRG, REU and CFG from dist into
D:\havoc. Keep the previous v15 build separately in D:\havoc-audio-baseline.
Keep each folder's PRG, REU and CFG together.

Use the same display, video standard, joystick and audio output for both runs.
Import havoc.cfg and check that CPU Speed is 64. The supplied configurations
are identical: four UltiSIDs, Left 1 / Right 1 panning, and the same mixer gains.
Leave the joystick's hardware autofire disabled.

For each build:
1. Reset, load that folder's havoc.reu at REU address zero, and run its havoc.prg.
2. Start a fresh game and press F to show the frame-rate readout. Let the stage
   music change finish before measuring combat.
3. Play the same combat section for at least 30 seconds, holding fire. Follow
   the same route and use bombs at the same points: double-tap fire within
   half a second. Include a section with many hits and overlapping explosions.
4. Note the normal FPS range and the lowest readings during sustained combat
   and bomb explosions. Record deaths, stage changes and music transitions
   separately. Repeat three times per build, alternating old/new order.

Listen for punch and clarity: a deep, snappy "pew" cannon that no longer
blurs under sustained fire, hull impacts as a heavy thud and crunch (not a
ricochet), ship explosions with a sharp crack followed 80 ms later by a
deep boom and fading rumble, and a bomb that hits at full chip volume for
three frames before settling. The barrel roll should be
a Doppler pass: a tone sinking as a whoosh swells and closes behind it, with
no rising "jump" sweep. The homing-missile-ready cue should be a low two-tone
lock-on, not a bright bell.
Check that the brief full-volume punches on hits, explosions and the bomb
sit well against the music and do not overwhelm it; the sustained level is
unchanged. Filter sweeps and effect durations still use 50 Hz timing. Check
left/right placement.

The complete soundtrack has twenty-one full-length songs. Check the dedicated
Citadel Core, Orbital Shipyard, Space Carrier, and Jump Gate recordings, and
confirm each stage song continues when its boss arrives. Losing all lives
should start Mission Failed; a fresh fire press should restore stage music.
Victory selects the sole mono Galactic Dash encoding. Listen for its 10 ms
right-channel delay, loop seam, and transitions to and from the stereo Hammond
Rocker title song. Mission Failed should loop while Game Over remains on screen;
returning to the title after victory should keep Galactic Dash playing.

At the title, hold CTRL and Commodore and tap DEL to enable soundtrack mode.
Confirm NOW PLAYING SOUNDTRACK... appears, then let all 21 full songs play:
Hammond Rocker, the eighteen stages in campaign order, Mission Failed, and
Galactic Dash. Check the automatic wrap to Hammond Rocker without a key press.
Comma must select the previous song and period the next, starting new songs
from the beginning and wrapping in both directions. Holding DEL, comma, or
period must act only once. Check rapid skips while a change is pending: the
latest selection wins, and returning to the still-playing song cancels the
pending switch without restarting that song.
Let attract mode begin and return to the title without restarting the song;
the music status and attract section name must remain readable with F on or off.
Start a run and confirm normal stage music returns and the menu mode survives.
Toggle soundtrack mode off: Hammond Rocker and NOW PLAYING TITLE MUSIC...
must return; comma and period must have no effect in title-only mode or gameplay.
After victory, return to the title and confirm Galactic Dash continues, with
NOW PLAYING VICTORY MUSIC... in title-only mode. Check the toggle still works.

DLT5 changes books roughly every 20.9 ms. Compare the selected previews listed
in target/digital-music/quality-experiments/quality-report.json with the previous
recordings at matched listening levels, focusing on attacks, cymbals,
sustained tones, quiet passages, and
segment/loop seams. Confirm that the more frequent book loads do not introduce
dropouts during combat or song changes. The quality report records coding-error
and peak measurements; those measurements do not replace this listening check.

The F readout averages frames over roughly one second; it cannot identify
every individual frame stall. Emulator SFX benchmarks and native DAC models
are separate measurements. This check includes real rendering, DAC music,
SID effects and the physical Ultimate together; it is the final check for
the sustained-combat slowdown reported on the machine.
