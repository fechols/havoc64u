# H.A.V.O.C. hardware and build notes

H.A.V.O.C. (Heavy Assault Vessel: Orbital Combat) targets the C64 Ultimate at 64 MHz with its built-in REU configured to 1 MB (1 MiB). The standard package in `dist/` uses two UltiSIDs; `dist/4sid/` adds two physical ARMSIDs for an enhanced arrangement. Import the chosen package's `havoc.cfg`, load its `havoc.reu` at REU address zero, then run its `havoc.prg`. Keep all three files from the same build and sound profile together. Older packages with 16 MiB images retain their original configurations.

The configuration selects the C64U turbo registers, 64 MHz CPU speed, disabled badline timing, and quiet bus operation. The runtime programs the turbo register at startup and checks that the setting is available. An ordinary 1 MHz C64 is not a supported execution target.

## Graphics and memory

The display is native VIC-II multicolor bitmap mode: 160×200 logical pixels drawn from the 16-color C64 palette. Each 4×8 logical-pixel cell has the global background colour plus three local colors; the background register shows the frame's clear colour (the sky on terrestrial stages), so unpainted pixels and ink of that colour cost no cell slot. Flat triangles paint two-bit colour codes straight into the cells; only a cell asked for a fourth other colour is quantized per pixel. There is no texture mapping.

The game draws into a single VIC bank. Every eight-row band is rasterized, given its HUD ink and fully packed in scratch RAM before a bounded copy publishes its bitmap, screen and color bytes. Publication proceeds immediately unless the raster is near that band's fetch window; it waits locally when necessary, with no whole-frame v-sync wait. A frame still rolls down the picture as it is produced. Six execution phases share one RAM window, with four ordinary enemy-profile SIM images, three boss-profile SIM images, and a terminal SIM image stored in REU. The audio player and interrupt handler remain resident. BOOT runs once; frames then run SIM, optional AUDIO page service, SCENERY, GEOMETRY, and PIXELS in order. The AUDIO overlay validates the complete score bank during startup and validates and prefetches requested pages during play; it is never called by the interrupt. Later AUDIO transfers omit the startup-only validator and directory parser.

Pressing F toggles a frame-rate readout in tenths at the top right of the picture, beside the boss panel, and the space bar pauses the flight: simulation time stands still behind a double-height PAUSED caption while frames keep rendering, stale joystick samples are discarded and the simulation deadline follows the clock so resuming does not replay the missed ticks. PIXELS counts completed frames over windows of at least fifty 50 Hz audio ticks and scans keyboard rows 7 (space) and 2 (F) once per frame with the sampling interrupt masked, returning CIA 1 port A to input immediately so joystick port 2 remains undisturbed. A column counts as a key only when it also reads high with no row selected, so a joystick in port 1 or an emulator driving port B cannot fake a press. The readout is ordinary HUD ink and is off at startup, so benchmark captures are unaffected unless it is switched on; pausing is ignored outside Playing and Transition.

Player damage reuses the bomb's resident impulse sequencer with a lighter profile: a `Hit` presentation event (outranked by a pending bomb, replaced by a reset) runs 240 ms of two-pixel horizontal kicks with at most one pixel of vertical motion and an 80 ms red border, packed into the same single byte as the bomb countdown with bit 7 selecting the profile. A hit never shortens a bomb shake in progress.

Bomb impacts use the VIC fine-scroll and border registers, adding no triangles or bitmap copies. A one-byte simulation event records a spent bomb independently of inventory rewards and the audio queue. SIM drains the latest bomb/reset event after catch-up and updates a resident sequencer with interrupts masked. Its 32-tick countdown runs on the existing 50 Hz clock; the raster interrupt at line 251 applies sixteen 40 ms samples. Horizontal kicks reach six physical pixels and vertical motion reaches three pixels either side of the normal Y-scroll value, tapering to rest over 640 ms. The border spends 80 ms each at white, yellow, orange, and red, then returns to black. Frame completion preserves active Y-scroll, and register writes preserve display-enable and bitmap-mode bits while keeping the raster comparison below line 256. Respawn and scene resets cancel the effect; scenic-flight seams preserve it, and a finishing bomb can continue through the results screen. Existing host snapshots contain only bitmap/screen/color bytes, so presentation-register effects are checked with native traces and VICE captures.

The bomb build passes 171 host tests per SID profile, with one existing fixture export ignored, and both production packages pass the PAL/NTSC weapon checks. Physical joystick gestures produce every shake phase, motion between frame publications, active retriggering, and neutral expiry; empty-inventory gestures produce no impulse. The observed countdowns last 623-639 ms, within the expected 20 ms clock quantization, with register updates following at the next raster interrupt. The two-SID ordinary-frame timing sample remains 5.493 FPS PAL / 5.766 FPS NTSC before and after the change. These short emulator samples do not establish physical-machine performance. Reports are under `target/vice/bomb-{2sid,4sid}-{pal,ntsc}`; the two-SID PAL run also covers controls, scenic streaming, and results-screen restarts.

Production companion screenshots have an existing screen/color-fetch defect with `$D031` bit 7 set: its matrix-fetch prefetch counter stays at four while badline stalls are disabled. Baseline captures made before the bomb change show the same corruption. Decoded bitmap/screen/color bytes and the VIC register traces validate rendering data and presentation timing, but final visual acceptance remains a physical C64 Ultimate check. The companion limitation is documented in the neighboring `doomc64u/docs/dual-image-game.md`.

The music reservation occupies `$0C0000-$0FFFFF` (256 KiB). Every code reservation stays within one 64 KiB bank, and PIXELS retains its exclusive bank. Logical overlay IDs remain unchanged; a linker-authored, 31-byte table in resident `.tables` maps IDs to REU bases in 4 KiB units. The loader resolves each base once before its bounded DMA loop.

Both sound profiles produce exactly 1,048,576-byte REU images. The allocated directories, common data, eighteen encounter/model banks, twenty-four `$4200` code slots and music reservation occupy 744,832 bytes in total, leaving 303,744 bytes (296.625 KiB) unreserved. These figures count reserved address ranges; the serialized code and music payloads use less than their reservations. The game only reads REU data during play; mutable game state stays in C64 RAM. The separate hardware timing probe writes solely to unreserved padding at `$0BFC00-$0BFFFF`.

Each composed SIM image has the same common prefix and one profile tail. The audited `encounter_step(Game*, AssetBank*)` entry owns actor movement, attacks, and the optional boss update. Shared code handles controls, progression, spawning, collisions, projectiles, and pickups. Stage identity is independent of this code profile: changing stages always refreshes the encounter metadata and model bank even when the next stage shares code. Native SIM reloads every frame because later phases reuse the window. A catch-up stage/profile change returns to the resident loader before another tick or render, preserving its existing fixed deadline. Enemy updates remain suspended during scenic flights.

SIM also loads a checked helper image from REU `$05A000` into `$8C00–$90FF`, a 1,280-byte execution allowance. This reuses retired startup-check space and the later geometry/pixel workspace; it does not replace the model cache or mission buffers. The resident loader restores it in two transfers before every SIM entry because later rendering phases overwrite that RAM. All general DMA transfers are capped at 1,024 bytes to bound audio IRQ latency. The IRQ never calls the helper image.

The 128-byte `OVL7` directory at REU `$000100` records twenty-four checked code images: existing phases, all eight SIM compositions, victory GEOMETRY, ten composed scenery images, and the SIM helper. The ascent and six orbital scenery records occupy bytes 88-115; helper fields remain at 84-87. The directory also authenticates SID configuration and the PRG, constants and startup code. Package ID `$534D0800` identifies the 1 MiB layout. It retains eighteen 2,048-byte `SMA4` model banks at `$0B0000-$0B8FFF`: each mesh descriptor keeps its vertex/face offsets and counts and adds a coordinate shift (byte 6, 0–3) and flags (byte 7, bit 0 = one-sided), and vertices are stored as three `i8` bytes in units of `1 << shift`. Every regular enemy, the player, the pickups and the medal fit shift 0 exactly; ordinary slot-4 hulls use shift 1 (their doubled coordinates are even, so they are exact too) and only the Dreadnought and Gate Guardian need shift 2. Faces keep three indices and a colour. The 64-byte `MDL2` directory at `$000040` retains version 2: magic at 0-3, stage count at 4, version at 5, bank size at 6, REU base at 8, eighteen checksums at 12-47, zero padding at 48-61, and checksum at 62. BOOT validates every bank, leaving stage 0 in the existing `$8400-$8BFF` cache. A stage change checks and copies the incoming bank in two 1,024-byte transfers. Same-stage frames and respawns retain the cache; model views never survive replacement.

The 64-byte main header contains eighteen encounter checksums at 20-55, zero padding at 56-61, and its checksum at 62. Eighteen 2,048-byte encounter allocations occupy `$002000-$00AFFF`; only their checked 512-byte prefixes enter the two gameplay buffers. `SMS2` keeps spawn records at byte 16 and the 62-record maximum. Bytes 8-15 hold the unchanged eight-byte `StageInfo`: enemy profile, music cue, crossing profile, boss kind, current/next/previous themes and one reserved zero byte. Complete campaign authoring remains host-only. Orbital encounters use ordinary movement profiles 0-3 and explicit three-dimensional hazard positions; ROCK flag bit 1 (`SMALL_DEBRIS = 2`) marks a shootable fragment. Unflagged rocks resist guns and missiles. A bomb destroys either size of active orbital ROCK, including wreckage, for 25 points without advancing enemy-kill rewards.

`SMA3` retains nine mesh descriptors at bytes 16–87 and three eight-byte enemy descriptors at 88–111. Header bytes 5, 6, and 7 identify stage, active enemy count, and boss kind. Ordinary stages expose three enemy slots; boss stages expose two escorts and reserve mesh slot 3 for boss side units. Mesh slot 4 contains the crawler, reactor, carrier hull, Dreadnought or Gate Guardian ring; slot 8 contains the 70-vertex / 116-triangle medal, including its bronze cockpit and twin engine relief. The tightest model bank uses 1,998 of 2,048 bytes. The player and pickup models remain shared; crystal and volcanic stages customize obstacle meshes. The 3,072-byte common bank contains the initial models and score directory. Runtime encounter fetches checksum their previously validated 512-byte prefixes.

Combat meshes are baked at their final size so the C64 keeps identity transforms. Regular enemies (slots 1–3 in ordinary stages, slots 1–2 in boss stages) use `assets::enemy_scaled`, three halves of the authored coordinates with truncation toward zero; their hit, ram and center descriptors scale the same way (shot radius 28, ram radius 27). The boss hull in slot 4 and the boss side units in slot 3 of boss stages keep `assets::BOSS_SCALE` (2×), as do the boss layout constants. Ground guns keep their −8 base through the pivot. Actors and pickups are no longer removed in front of the camera: `game::ACTOR_RETIRE_Z` keeps every actor alive and submitted until the deepest actor mesh is entirely behind the near plane, where the renderer rejects every face; `tests/assets.rs` checks that invariant against every stage's model bank. Flat pickups and hostile shots retire at the near plane itself. That plane (`render::NEAR`) now sits 8 units from the eye instead of 32: a vertex is on-screen only when |x| < z and |y| < 0.75 z, so geometry can be cut visibly only inside that small pyramid, and passing ships leave the screen edges before they reach it. Every actor, terrain (|x| ≤ 640) and scenery coordinate still projects inside the ±8191 clamp at that depth, and `projection::project_uncached` bounds |y| to 30,000 at unshifted depths, where larger values already saturate, so the i32 vertical product cannot overflow.

Ordinary terrestrial frames transfer only `__phase_scenery_terrain_size`, omitting both space helpers and decoration tails. The relocation audit accepts only the reviewed opaque entries and rejects cross-profile references. Every borrowed asset view ends before its DMA destination is reused.

Once black, scenery and ship emission stop. The alternate GEOMETRY tail replaces combat drawing. The terminal SIM tail handles title/menu changes, respawning, scenic crossings, Victory and GameOver; ordinary attract ticks use the scene's enemy profile, returning to terminal SIM for dismissal or the next preview. Space Carrier keeps three alternating hull rows and retires each only after its rear geometry has passed the near plane.

No 32-bit arithmetic survives in the target build. The millisecond clock (`CLOCK_MS`, `InputSample::ms`, the `Controls` release and tap instants, `Runtime::sim_ms`) is 16 bits and wraps every 65.5 s: every window it serves is under two seconds, `Controls::sample` holds the release instant within the two-second charge window and disarms a tap older than the 500 ms double-tap window, and a paused frame still samples the held buttons once (the clock keeps running while the simulation stands still), so no remembered instant can look recent again after a wrap; `tools/bench-native.py` writes the two-byte clock and `tools/vice-smoke.py` reads two-byte sample stamps. `Hud::distance` is the section-local distance (`stage_ticks × 8`, at most 30,000), the only form the HUD uses; the monitor-visible `GAME_DISTANCE` keeps the campaign total as `(section × 1,875) << 4 + local`. The HUD bars, the victory reveal's easing (`remaining × 64 / 100`), the wildlife slope (the bounded division's Q8 fraction times a Q8 product) and the music-kernel image offsets (`cue × slot` fits 16 bits) use 16-bit arithmetic. The 380-byte `__udivsi3`, 219-byte `__divsi3` and 103-byte `__mulsi3` compiler helpers are no longer linked; with the smaller clock code (`computed_charge` 202 → 105 bytes, `Game::hud` 533 → 309, the IRQ body −44) resident code shrank from 10,018 to 8,844 bytes, the SIM prefix by 277 bytes, the PIXELS prefix by 147 and `Game` to 621 bytes. The score stays 32-bit: a full run's boss bonuses alone exceed 65,535, and its adds and digit conversion link no helpers.

Startup disables both ROMs before clearing BSS. The custom linker discards the SDK's `.init.250` PETSCII case-mode hook because it calls KERNAL through vector RAM now owned by game globals. The game's data-copy hook uses `.init.251`; the build audit verifies that startup calls only BSS clearing, data copying, and `main`.

A fatal startup or runtime error stops playback and displays a text diagnostic in screen A. This terminal error path restores normal VIC badline timing with `$D031=$0F`, allowing the character display to refresh normally; gameplay uses the configured disabled-badline mode.

SCENERY, GEOMETRY, and PIXELS bank out I/O to access the triangle command array. SCENERY writes up to 96 raw, camera-space records of 20 bytes each into its existing tail at `$D880` through `$DFFF`; no additional resident staging allocation is needed. Each record holds three signed 16-bit XYZ vertices, color, and flags. GEOMETRY reads a record before projecting it, checks that emitted commands cannot overwrite unread records, and then adds foreground geometry. It rotates the projected scenery prefix behind the foreground before stable sorting, preserving the host renderer's foreground-first order at equal depth. The foreground face allowance is `min(174, 233 - raw scenery count)`, leaving near-plane clipping room in the 256-command array.

GEOMETRY clears and uses a 640-byte vertex cache inside the color-band workspace at `$8C00`. Every triangle reaches the resident renderer through `fast_clip_triangle` (`target-c64/asm/emit.S`, the native body of the renderer's `GeometrySink::triangle`): corners all at or behind the near plane go to `fast_emit_triangle`, corners all in front are dropped, and a mixed triangle is clipped against z = 8 exactly like the host's `near_intersection` (Q15 interpolation by the bounded division, the 17-bit coordinate difference times t through quarter-square partials, the arithmetic shift) into a three- or four-corner polygon. `fast_emit_triangle` looks each corner up in the cache by a byte mix of its six coordinate bytes (the cache is a memo keyed by the whole point, so the index need not match the host's arithmetic hash), projects misses in place, culls by the signed area, rejects triangles off one screen edge, computes the depth third without a division (`85 h + (h + l) / 3` with a 256-entry thirds table) and writes the 16-byte command. Once all vertices are projected, the stable depth sort (`fast_depth_sort`, `target-c64/asm/sort.S`) reuses the 1 KiB at `$8C00–$8FFF` for the two key bytes of every command, an intermediate index list and 256 bucket counts: two least-significant-digit counting passes over the byte key `!(depth ^ $8000)`, scattered from the last command down so equal depths keep their order. It handles all 256 commands and does not move them: the commands stay in emission order and the sorted order — one command index per position — goes to `$7600–$76FF`, which PIXELS reads while preparing the raster heads. Ties follow the logical order `scenery_last` records (the foreground staged after the scenery first, then the scenery prefix, then anything added later, as the host's physical rotation yields); `tools/bench-native.py` reads the commands through the order so the geometry hash is unchanged. Mesh transforms calculate each active axis's sine and cosine once per object and skip identity scaling, preserving the original separate Q8 products and rounding. No projection-cache references survive sorting, and the IRQ never uses this workspace. Raw scenery records never survive to the next frame because projection reuses their command bytes.

The resident `mul_q8` is an assembly kernel (`fast_mul_q8`): it multiplies the operand magnitudes through unsigned 8x8 partial products from the quarter-square table BOOT builds at `$C000` (`Q[n] = floor(n*n/4)`, 512 low bytes then 512 high bytes) and rounds a negative product toward minus infinity, exactly like the reference `((a as i32 * b as i32) >> 8) as i16`. GEOMETRY composes each mesh instance's yaw, pitch and roll into a 3×3 Q8 matrix once (`fast_mesh_matrix`, the body of `RotationMatrix::compose`, entries clamped to ±256) and rotates every stored `i8` vertex with `fast_rotate_vertex`: nine quarter-square products with zero entries skipped, a 24-bit sum and one arithmetic shift by `8 − mesh shift (+1 for half-size debris)`. Projection is `fast_project` (`target-c64/asm/project.S`), the body of `projection::project_reference`: z normalised to 128..255 with a signed exponent, one 16×16 product per axis against the resident `PROJECT_X`/`PROJECT_Y` tables (`80·32768/d`, `133·32768/d`), a byte-aligned shift and the ±8191 clamps; `project_cached` keeps the 64-entry hash cache around it with shifted adds instead of 16-bit multiplies. `tools/test-multiply-kernel.py` and `tools/test-projection-kernel.py` check every kernel against its reference in `mos-sim`, and `target-c64/build.rs` runs `tools/branch-audit.py` on every assembled object so a silently wrapped conditional branch fails the build.

PIXELS prepares a sixteen-byte persistent raster head at `$EF20` for each sorted position, reading the command through the sorted order (the command array is not consumed). `fast_prepare_triangle` keeps the host's exact Q16 arithmetic but takes a fast path for the usual edge (dy ≤ 255 and |dx| ≤ 255): a 24-bit restoring division by the 8-bit dy, the row-centre fraction `16384 / dy` from a table when the start row is on screen, and two quarter-square products; native positions carry +32767 so a row's column is simply the high word. Twelve-byte tails at `$E000` and ordered bit masks at `$EC00` identify the triangles touching each band. Edge positions advance sequentially in Q16 across all 25 bands, including bands whose painting can be skipped; edges are not recomputed from scratch for every band. The band driver is assembly (`fast_raster_band`): it scans the band's ordered mask and derives each marked triangle's head and tail addresses from its sorted position; the row walker copies the head into zero page (`$D0–$DF`), splits the band's rows into the segment before the middle row and the segment from it (so no row tests for the middle), clamps both columns from the high words, hands every nonempty span to the cell writer and writes the positions back when it leaves the band, except from the triangle's last band. Spans do not paint pixels: the cell writer (`target-c64/asm/cells.S`, the native body of `havoc::cells::CellBand`) writes two-bit colour codes straight into a row-major 8×40 table of cell bytes while a three-slot palette per cell hands out the hardware colours on first use (the span loops take a free slot inline). Code 0 is the VIC background, which PIXELS sets to the frame's clear colour at the start of every frame, so the clear colour and every unpainted pixel need no slot; the slots store keys (colour XOR the clear colour), which makes the clear colour key 0 like a free slot and keeps every fast path's zero test free of a comparison. A slot whose colour no longer appears in any of the cell's eight rows is recycled; a cell asked for a genuine fourth other colour is expanded into the pixel band at `$8C00` and painted per pixel from then on (its palette is poisoned with `$FF` so the span fast paths route every later write there; whole cell rows of plain or terrain ink take unrolled pixel paths). Terrain-clear coverage lives in a parallel table of nibbles; exterior terrain skips covered pixels; HUD ink marks its slot so the expanded pixels carry the quantizer's priority bit. `fast_cell_stage` transposes the 320 code bytes into the consumed coverage table and writes the 80 attribute bytes over the consumed HUD-slot and overflow tables (XORing the keys back; a free slot shows the clear colour). Each overflow flag is consumed before its byte is replaced. Only overflowed cells pass through the classic per-pixel quantizer (`fast_pack_cell`); their staged attributes are first seeded with the visible preceding values so its palette-retention behavior stays identical. Staging invalidates the coverage-clean marker, ensuring the next band clears the reused bytes. Before the background carried the clear colour, cells crossed by terrain while still showing sky spilled into the pixel form 40–60 times per frame (about 5 ms); with it they rarely do. Decoded pixels are checked against the host reference with the background (`tools/bench-native.py --pixel-identity` records `$D021` as byte 10,000 of a capture). The HUD, text and ambient dust draw through the same `Canvas` trait (`span`, `plot`, `glyph`) as the host reference.

`fast_pack_cell` (`target-c64/asm/pack.S`) is the only remaining per-pixel quantizer: it ranks a spilled cell's colours (count, retained slot, HUD priority), keeps retained colours in their preceding slots, maps omitted colours to the nearest chosen colour or the background, and writes the eight bitmap bytes and two attribute bytes in place. `tools/test-pack-kernel.py` checks it byte for byte against `render::pack_band` over 256 bands (random, exact-colour, row-uniform and rendered-scene bands with black and coloured backgrounds). The band packer and its partial-cell sampling table at `$7700` are gone; `$7700–$774F` now hold the cell writer's HUD-slot and overflow flags.

Each of the ten terrestrial visual themes uses five horizon triangles and nine cloud facets, decoded from compact signed-byte descriptors in SCENERY. The backdrop is authored in camera space; crossings interpolate the outgoing and incoming arrangements. Horizons use the neutral gameplay camera height of 40. The extended horizon bases cover the moving terrain even at camera extremes. Each theme has its own horizon and cloud arrangement and palette. Outdoor clouds and horizons occupy separate bands, so each 4-by-8 cell contains at most three colours besides the sky (the background) despite the larger palette across the complete backdrop. Crystal Cave reuses the fourteen faces for walls, roof, and hanging crystals, sharing three such colors even where they overlap. Ashen Wastes limits its scenery and rock obstacles to orange, charcoal, and black, leaving a cell slot available to an isolated red projectile.

Backdrop faces and the orbital starfield carry the `STATIC_SKY` flag, also while a crossing blends them. Since every band is repainted every frame, the flag no longer selects cached bands; GEOMETRY applies a slight sky parallax to flagged records as it replays staged scenery (`Renderer::project_scenery`): the far sky moves opposite to the ship by its full sideways offset and half its height (`Game::sky_parallax`), which perspective reduces to a few pixels at the horizon and slightly more for the nearer clouds. The host reference (`Game::render_scenery`) applies the identical shift before the sink, so staged and direct host renders stay pixel-identical. Title and map tours keep their authored framing; planets, terrain and all foreground geometry never shift. No sky band cache or validity mask remains.

Attract mode uses the same simulation, section cache, geometry, and raster pipeline as gameplay. Title inactivity and each section preview last 750 fixed 20 ms steps on both PAL and NTSC. A latched activity bit preserves joystick pulses between simulation steps; scene resets consume the dismissal input and clear encounters and gestures. Attract frames omit the ship, reticle, and cockpit and paint all 25 bands. The small phase-independent Q8 multiply helper lives in resident code to accommodate the expanded game-state layout. Attract scenes select the same area cues as gameplay.

Playing mode inserts a 400-tick scenic flight at each of seventeen boundaries. Each stage has 3,750 combat ticks: eighteen stages plus crossings total 74,300 ticks, or 24:46 before deaths and boss holds. Native progress uses a stage-local `u16 stage_ticks` plus the stage ID; aggregate HUD/report distance is derived with 32-bit arithmetic. Scenery uses local crossing coordinates so campaign length cannot overflow its old absolute 16-bit clock. Bosses enter at local tick 1,000 in stages 4, 8 and 12, and tick 2,500 in stages 17 and 18. Unfinished bosses hold progress at tick 3,750 while combat continues. The outgoing checkpoint remains active through a crossing; its successor begins only at exit.

Player controls, charge and double-tap history, player projectiles, and explosions remain live during scenic flights. Entry removes outgoing actors, pickups, and hostile shots before processing controls; enemy spawning, boss simulation, collisions, and damage are suspended. Bombs retain their inventory cost. Defeating any boss immediately restores shield 100 and bombs 3, once; its subsequent crossing preserves those resources and adds one life up to three. Other crossings preserve resources. Decorations have no actor, collision, or targeting state. The incoming checkpoint and music begin on exit, with encounter processing on the following tick. The existing two-slot cache prefetches that incoming stage during the crossing.

All bosses start with 40 HP per side unit and 120 core HP; the HUD displays the rounded-up percentage of their 200-point total. Shield drops are eligible every eighth enemy kill and restore 60 points capped at 100; bomb-carrier priority and full pickup-pool behavior are unchanged. All active pickup types share the original shield attraction range and speed. Rocks and bosses do not advance ordinary enemy-kill pickup or bomb rewards.

The assembly IRQ wrapper preserves the processor-port setting, maps I/O back in, saves the compiler registers and static scratch frames, switches to the IRQ stack, and restores everything before returning. This also protects interrupted arithmetic helpers that LLVM-MOS gives static local storage. Compile-time guards, the linker, and the post-link layout checker enforce the reserved boundaries.

### Guarded band publication

`publish_staged_band` (`target-c64/asm/publish.S`) copies one completed band:
320 bitmap bytes from `$9340`, 40 screen bytes from `$7700`, and 40 color
bytes from `$7728`. There is no additional framebuffer. These are consumed
coverage, HUD-slot and overflow tables, and `fast_cell_stage` marks coverage
dirty so the next band clears it before drawing.

For band `b`, the publisher excludes raster lines `[40 + 8b, 64 + 8b)`.
This covers the union of all Y-scroll fetch windows, eight leading lines
for the bounded copy and a trailing margin. It publishes immediately outside
that interval and polls until it ends otherwise: the nominal maximum wait
is 24 lines (about 1.5 ms), plus polling and interrupt overhead, rather than
a refresh period. `$D011` high / `$D012` low / `$D011` high
sampling rejects ninth-bit rollover; a second sample with IRQs masked closes
the race between checking and copying. IRQs stay enabled while waiting and
quantizing. The final recheck and copy alone mask IRQs, restoring the caller's
flags afterward. Both the conservative instruction/I/O model and native
benchmark traces check a seven-scanline bound. The model deliberately charges
an extra bus cycle for every VIC read and color store; physical turbo timing
remains to be measured.

Publication uses eight-byte bitmap loops and four-cell attribute loops.
The profiling detail markers distinguish staging/quantization (3), copying
and scheduling overhead (4), and raster polling (9). The benchmark also traces
the successful masked guard through the last visible store, accounting for
failed guards separately. Its checks reject missing copies or an overlong
protected section. The tests execute assembled publisher bytes across PAL/NTSC
boundaries, IRQ arrivals and raster rollover, and compare staged output with
the existing renderer reference.

The picture still updates progressively, and `$D021` still changes at the
start of PIXELS. Global background transitions can recolor old code-zero
pixels before their bands are replaced; this change addresses local
bitmap/attribute mismatches, not atomic whole-frame or background changes.

## SID configurations

The two executables are selected at build time with Cargo's `four-sid` feature. There is no runtime hardware probe or profile menu. Both configurations disable range splitting and automatic address mirroring, giving every enabled SID a separate address. The standard configuration leaves both physical sockets unmapped and muted.

| Chip | Address | Two-SID package | Four-SID package | Pan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UltiSID 1 | `$D400` | 0 dB; two music + one effect voice | Same | Left 2 |
| UltiSID 2 | `$D420` | 0 dB; two music + one effect voice | Same | Right 2 |
| Physical socket 1 | `$D440` | Unmapped, muted | −6 dB; three music voices | Left 2 |
| Physical socket 2 | `$D460` | Unmapped, muted | −6 dB; three music voices | Right 2 |

The extra pair is intended for ARMSIDs configured for 8580 behavior. Set that behavior on the ARMSIDs themselves: the game's configuration supplies socket addressing, gain, and pan, not ARMSID firmware settings. The −6 dB socket gain is the initial balance for the added orchestration. Addressing and mixer controls are described in the [Ultimate MultiSID guide](https://1541u-documentation.readthedocs.io/en/latest/config/multi_sid.html).

Both UltiSIDs use the same `8580 Lo` filter curve, `High` resonance setting, and `8580` combined-waveform behavior. Music owns their filters; the reserved third effects voices bypass them. All three voices on each added physical SID are available to music. The engine uses oscillator waveforms, ADSR envelopes, noise, and, on the extra physical SIDs, ring modulation of triangle voices by their chip's noise oscillator. Per-song kernels sweep the filter cutoff, pulse widths and pitches between rows. SID volume remains constant during playback; there are no digital samples or volume-register DAC effects.

The Ultimate Audio mapping at `$DF20–$DFFF` is disabled, and both sampler channels are muted. The firmware's `Digis Level=Medium` setting is retained but is not used for sampled playback. Mixer panning applies to HDMI and AV output, not the physical SID-TAP header. The built-in SID player's autoconfiguration can override mixer settings, so re-import the game configuration after using that player. See the [official mixer documentation](https://1541u-documentation.readthedocs.io/en/latest/config/audio_mixer.html).

## Paged melodic score

The score runs at 50 Hz from CIA1 Timer A independently of rendering, with PAL/NTSC timer periods and frequency tables. Twenty-one cues preserve the original thirteen arrangements and add six mission themes, an ascent cue and the Gate Guardian encounter. The four core parts remain identical in both profiles; baking strips only the optional six-part orchestration for two SIDs. The additional cues reuse the existing paged player and require no larger IRQ page cache.

Each score page contains one bar of sixteen sixteenth-note rows. Cues carry their own checked row duration. The score allocation is `$0C0000-$0FFFFF` (256 KiB), with 32-bit bank-relative offsets; package `assets.txt` records the exact used byte count for its sound profile. Track names, arrangements and row timing are authored in [src/soundtrack.rs](../src/soundtrack.rs).

The resident engine holds the active page, SID register shadows, two effects states, and a small event queue. The foreground AUDIO overlay fills a separate next-page buffer and publishes it while interrupts are masked. The buffer is not modified until the player consumes it. The IRQ copies a ready page at the bar boundary and requests its successor; it performs no REU access. If the next page is late, playback waits before the boundary's release tick, preserving held music and continuing effects. `MUSIC_UNDERRUNS` counts each incident. Cue changes request page zero and insert a complete gate-low tick before the new cue starts.

Startup also caches the twenty-one validated `CueInfo` descriptors in 168 bytes of resident low-RAM `.bss`. This array lies outside the compiler scratch region saved by the IRQ, and only foreground AUDIO code reads it. Its entries become available after the complete bank scan succeeds and remain unchanged during play. Page requests use these cached offsets, lengths and tempos, avoiding repeated REU directory transfers. The IRQ engine and its active/next page capacities remain unchanged.

Historical OST-build MOS layouts used 177 bytes for the two-SID engine plus a 96-byte next page, or 355 bytes for the four-SID engine plus a 224-byte next page: **273 and 579 bytes total**. Seven further bytes hold the loader checksum, validation flag, and exported music diagnostics. The two-SID build places both audio objects inside the existing 432-byte `$CE00–$CFAF` allocation. The four-SID engine uses that allocation and places its next page at `$0720–$07FF`; compiler scratch ends at `$05CD`. The foreground loader reuses the retired band workspace for its directory. That OST revision had 710 bytes of resident headroom in the standard build and 696 in the enhanced build, with one spare byte in GEOMETRY. These are historical figures, not the six-overlay scenic build limits. The memory maps in each package record the actual target sizes; host ABI sizes can differ.

The historical OST ELF symbols account for **2,699 / 2,707 bytes of resident player code** in the two/four-SID builds: music transport, effects, register writes, pitch conversion, page requests, and the audio-tick wrapper. This excludes shared arithmetic helpers, constants, and the common IRQ wrapper. The foreground AUDIO overlay is 2,859 / 2,881 bytes and reuses the existing overlay window; it adds no separate resident code allocation. Its loader and validators are unreachable from the IRQ. Four-SID routing tables avoid a per-note divide, keeping the extra six voices inexpensive in code size.

The bank format is `SMU3`. Its 256-byte header records SID/voice profile, total length, page size, and twenty-one eight-byte cue entries; the directory can represent thirty cues without another resize. Entries contain bank-relative offset, page count, row duration and cue checksum. The common asset bank stores a matching header copy. `SPG2` pages keep their layout: a 12-byte header, five instrument bytes per voice, and sixteen rows of four or ten events, totaling 96/224 bytes for two/four SIDs. Header byte 9 is the bar's filter cutoff target (SID register 22); zero holds the filter, so the historical pages remain byte-identical. Each instrument's control byte carries one waveform plus optional sync (`$02`) and ring-modulation (`$04`) bits; ring modulation is only accepted on triangle voices, and the player writes the byte unchanged with the gate bit, so the colours cost no resident code. Unused bytes are zero.

Events are `0` for rest, `1` for hold, MIDI notes `24..95` for an articulated attack, or a note with bit `$80` set for legato. Holds preserve the gate and pitch; legato changes pitch without releasing the gate. A new attack or rest releases the preceding note one 20 ms tick before its boundary. Page publication rejects a hold or legato opening when switching cues or changing that voice's instrument. For *Lost Wing*, an attack plus hold at nine ticks per row reproduces each old 18-tick note, followed by two resting rows; its full loop remains 2,304 ticks, or 46.08 seconds.

The produced score keeps the four core parts in both profiles. The electronic and orbital cues add Jeroen Tel-style production entirely in the page data: lead and bass pulse widths step across every eight-bar phrase; voice six echoes the lead a dotted eighth (or an eighth) later from the opposite UltiSID in the four-SID ensemble; the orbital drum kit uses real noise decays, slurred falling snares, a tuned sub-octave kick body, phrase-end rolls and section crashes; basses walk chromatically into each chord change; strings swell through the second and final eights and drop out for a two-bar breakdown before each reprise; *Slingshot Through the Belt*, *Rivets in Orbit* and *Carrier Overdrive* lift their final sections a whole tone with newly written melodies; and *Silver Fractures*, *Beyond the Blue Threshold*, *Ashes and Steel* and *Rivets in Orbit* ring-modulate bells and anvils on the extra SIDs. *Lost Wing* is untouched. `tests/soundtrack.rs` pins every cue's published checksum in both profiles.

Header and page checksums treat their own two-byte fields as zero. Whole-cue checksums cover the stored pages including their checksums. Before the first cue plays, the foreground validator checks the header signature, all directory bounds, contiguous cue spans, profile fields, instrument/note ranges, tempos, page indices, next-page links, padding, and checksums. It also verifies the final bank extent before publishing the cached descriptors. Every subsequently fetched page is validated again before publication, including its checksum, cue/page identity, tempo and next-page link. Runtime uses the validated cached descriptors as its directory authority; it does not reread the REU header. The first AUDIO load includes the validator and parser, while later loads stop at `__phase_audio_runtime_size`. The `OVL7` code directory still checks the complete AUDIO image alongside the other executable images and matching PRG, constants, startup code, and SID profile.

## Per-song music kernels

Each cue except *Lost Wing* ships its own modulation kernel: a small piece of 6502 code, written in Rust in [src/audio/kernels.rs](../src/audio/kernels.rs), that the IRQ calls once per tick after the transport has written the row. Kernels are composed from shared building blocks — filter glide towards the bar's authored target, the bass-triggered "wah" envelope, triangle pulse-width shimmer, delayed vibrato, portamento into slurred notes, a chord-safe double-time arpeggio that flutters between consecutive authored pitches, a tick-rate drum program that turns kicks into sliding pulse bodies and lets snares fall, and a modulator sweep for the ring-modulated bells — with the constants baked per song. They touch SID registers only through the engine's deduplicating writes, read the row the transport already wrote, keep all state in a sixteen-byte `scratch` that clears at every loop start, and never touch the reserved effects voices, routing or volume. Both sound profiles run them; the standard package gains the same expression on its four core voices.

On the target every kernel is linked at the same resident slot (`$7200–$74FF`, 768 bytes) and stored as a separate REU image behind an `SMK1` directory at `$0B9000`: a 16-byte header with its own checksum, one `size, checksum` entry per cue, and a whole-directory checksum. The host `pack-overlays.py` writes the images from the linked ELF together with the `OVL7` code images, so the existing PRG/REU pairing check covers them. Startup verifies the complete directory and every image before `MUSIC_VALIDATED`; at each cue change the foreground AUDIO overlay marks the slot invalid under an IRQ mask, fetches the header and the cue's entry into the slot, copies and re-checksums the image (every transfer stays far below the 1 KiB DMA bound), and only then re-enables the slot for that cue. The IRQ calls the slot only while the loaded kernel belongs to the playing cue, so a changed cue's final bar is never coloured by its successor's kernel. The two/four-SID images are 275–748 bytes; the costliest kernel measures about 3,400 cycles per tick on the 6502 model, under 55 µs at 64 MHz. The slot reuses half of the former 1 KiB IRQ software stack, whose high-water mark measured three bytes across five native cue loops; `tools/check-layout.py` and `tools/check-overlays.py` verify the slot placement, every image size, and that kernels reference only resident code and data. The `music-kernels` Cargo feature (on by default in `target-c64`) can be disabled to ship the plain transport.

`tools/test-music-kernel.py` replays the linked kernel images on a Python 6502 model against fixtures from `cargo run --release --features music-kernels -- kernel-fixtures` (add `--features four-sid` for the enhanced profile): 1,181 engine states per profile, each requiring identical engine bytes and SID writes to the host run, no stores outside the engine, SID, zero-page, stack and static-frame regions, and a balanced hardware stack. `tools/test-corrupt-package.py` includes a corrupted kernel directory, entry and image, which boot rejects with `INVALID MUSIC KERNEL`.

`python3 tools/test-game-math-kernel.py` builds the production swept-depth assembly and a frozen Rust reference under the configured Linux/WSL toolchain. It checks 6,658 native simulator cases and records sizes and an identical 2,048-call timing harness in `target/game-math-test/report.json`. The native `memcpy.S` replaces the Rust compiler's larger memory-copy routine and its static scratch frame. It follows the LLVM-MOS C ABI and uses only caller-clobbered registers. `python3 tools/test-memcpy-kernel.py` runs 1,297 native 6502 simulator cases against an independent volatile byte-copy loop, covering empty copies, page boundaries, unaligned addresses, returned pointers, and source/destination guards. Its generated report lives under `target/memcpy-test`.

Unscaled 3-by-5 text that fits completely within one eight-row band uses a bounded path: Rust checks the whole label once, then calls `fast_cell_glyph` with each glyph's five font rows, which paints at most two cell masks per row. Clipped or split-band text retains the general renderer, which paints each lit glyph pixel as a `scale`-wide span. Host tests compare this path with `draw_text_scaled` for all 256 character bytes and clipping boundaries; `python3 tools/test-cells-kernel.py` checks the glyph, span and plot kernels against the C transcription of the reference.

The other native kernels preserve host reference behavior: `sort.S` implements stable far-to-near radix ordering (writing the order table rather than moving commands), `raster.S` advances prepared edges and hands spans to the cell writer, and `pack.S` quantizes the overflowed cells. The compact sort kernel uses the resident table region; the raster walker and preparation kernel use PIXELS, leaving the fixed overlay boundaries intact. Their simulator checks compare bytes against independent references and verify memory guards (`tools/test-sort-kernel.py` runs the production Rust entry through 416 sorts, with and without scenery segments; `tools/test-clip-kernel.py` 1,584 triangles through the clipping kernel). Host geometry tests compare cached transforms with the original per-vertex arithmetic. These correctness checks do not substitute for target timing measurements.

`missile.S` (`fast_steer_missile`, in every SIM image) is the native body of `Game::steer_missile`: it keeps a valid target lock or randomly acquires an eligible actor, live boss turret, or exposed core ahead, then applies the per-axis guidance (`missile_velocity`) with the release build's wrapping 16-bit product, truncating division and ±12 clamp. Its 21-byte argument record contains actor and descriptor addresses, shot position, boss state, dx/dy, target ID and random state. The 1,002-byte kernel uses `$9140–$917D`, GEOMETRY scratch that is idle during SIM. Game stores 24 four-bit target locks in twelve bytes plus one random-state byte: native state is 631 bytes (including the ten-byte flight record), within the 640-byte limit. Actor-slot reuse invalidates locks before replacing the actor. The right-shift `$B8` LFSR starts at `$A5`; one-based high-nibble rejection gives equal candidate ranks over a complete accepted cycle. Retained locks and zero/one-candidate searches consume no random draws. Full scene resets restore the seed; checkpoint respawns clear locks and preserve random state. `python3 tools/test-missile-kernel.py` compares the kernel with a C transcription of the host over 12,852 cases, checking target ID, RNG state, aim point, closing speed and dx/dy, including all byte seeds, candidate counts, retained locks, boss states and wrapping-coordinate extremes. The composed SIM images retain 40 bytes of room after the lock-management changes.

`shots.S` (`fast_shot_scan`, in every SIM image) is the native body of `Game::shot_target`, the scan `Game::update_shots` runs for every moved player shot and missile: in slot order it selects each live actor's radius (18 for small debris, 35 for rocks and buildings, else the descriptor's `shot_radius`) and centre offset, then applies `swept_z` over the move and `close_xy` with the release build's wrapping differences, and returns the first hit slot or `$FF`. Its arguments arrive in registers (old depth in A/X, then the position, actor and descriptor addresses and the descriptor count in `rc2–rc8`), it keeps its state in caller-saved `rc2–rc19` and tests the lateral distance first, so a slot more than 255 units to the side costs about 70 cycles and no radius lookup. The compiled scan was about 400 cycles per live slot per shot; the kernel is 441 bytes and 0.7–2.6 k cycles per shot for twelve empty to twelve nearby slots (the missiles fixture's SIM slice fell 21.7 → 16.8 ms at ten ticks; the largest SIM profile keeps 528 bytes of room). `python3 tools/test-shots-kernel.py` compares it with a C transcription of the host loop over 56,564 scenes: every edge of every kind class's hit volume on both sides in both sweep directions, slot-order ties, the wrapping corners and random scenes with random descriptors.

`flight.S` (`fast_flight_step`, resident) is the native body of `Game::move_player`, the player's assisted flight model (`src/game/flight.rs`, a Q8 port of havoc2's aircraft behaviour at the 50 Hz tick). `Game` keeps the integer position in `player` and a ten-byte `Flight` record directly after it: the two position fractions, the Q8 velocities, the bank and the pitch (Q8 of 256ths of a turn; their high bytes are the mesh angles). Each tick the stick sets a target velocity (±3.0 units per tick sideways, ±2.75 vertically, plus a 2.5 push in the roll's direction while the hull spins); the velocity moves 3/32 of the way to it (a 213 ms time constant) by the shifted magnitude of the error and at least one unit while any error remains, so every state settles exactly; a soft wall then bounds it by `(edge + 7 − q) >> 3` on the near side of the ±100 × ±65 lane, so the ship decelerates exponentially into the former hard limits, reaches them exactly and never crosses them; the bank target is −16 × the lateral acceleration (none for a single settling unit) clamped to ±20/256 of a turn, the pitch target −4 × the vertical target, and both angles follow with an eighth of their error per tick. Negative mesh angles lower the right wing, so a right turn banks negative and the 25-tick barrel roll's linear spin runs negative for a right roll too (`Game::player_appearance` adds the spin to the bank, so the end of the full turn is continuous with the banked hull). The arguments arrive in registers (stick bits in A, push in X, the player and flight addresses in `rc2–rc5`), the state lives in caller-saved `rc6–rc18`, and the kernel is 638 bytes and about 1.9 k cycles per tick (0.03 ms); it replaced a 493-byte compiled routine, and the resident Q8 multiply and bounded division kernels moved to the `$9900` table page to make room. `python3 tools/test-flight-kernel.py` compares it byte for byte with a C transcription of the host reference over 147,850 steps: every stick pattern with every push held and released from rest and the lane's corners, and random states (stale fractions, positions outside the lane, extreme velocities and angles) as single steps and continuing flights.

## Terrain visibility coverage

The player hull/exhaust, ordinary-stage tracked guns and Fortress bunkers use the
`TERRAIN_CLEAR_GEOMETRY` command flag (`0x20`). Their authored geometry is proven
to lie in front of every exterior terrain face along the camera ray. The flag
survives near clipping and preparation without changing the 16-byte command or
12-byte tail. Drawing these faces sets pixel bit `0x40`; ordinary foreground
writes replace the color while retaining that coverage, and tagged exterior
terrain skips covered pixels. Canyon wall batteries, boss turrets, buildings,
rocks, effects and other actors keep normal foreground ordering.

The hull stays inside X -136..137 and above world Y -103 for all 256 roll angles;
tracked guns fit the same minimum 145-unit lane. Fortress bunkers reach X +/-158
but stay at Y >= -98, above the conservative campaign shoulder envelope
`495*(y+105) = 240*(abs(x)-145)`. Authored ground enemies spawn after their incoming
terrain blend finishes. Transitions clear actors, and attract previews use their
stage's terrain style. Tests cover all eight stages with ordinary ground enemies,
the original twelve terrain sequences and eleven terrestrial crossings, plus orbital coverage tests. They enforce these bounds,
spawn timing and terrain separation. Do not assign this flag to arbitrary
meshes or below-ground objects without establishing the same visibility proof.

Only lane, shoulder, outer-cap and runway strips carry `EXTERIOR_TERRAIN` (`0x40`
in commands/tails). Wildlife and transport strips remain ordinary geometry. The
private scenery triangle helper temporarily packs the terrain tag above its
color nibble to retain a compact native calling convention, then emits separate
color and geometry flags to the sink.

The raster walker uses one sequential-phase byte at `$77AB` to remember whether
a protected span has painted the current band. Each band resets it, including
hidden and empty bands. Preparation and packing borrow overlapping scratch only
before and after rasterization; the IRQ never accesses it. Bands without protected
coverage retain the ordinary constant-color fill. Both packers ignore pixel bit 6;
HUD priority remains bit 7. There is no additional framebuffer or triangle storage.

The raster walker now resides in PIXELS. The conservative static-sky scan and the
pure scenery blend-weight helper occupy resident table-code space, keeping all
phase ownership and memory-limit checks active. The coverage rule does not replace
the general average-depth painter sort for other overlapping geometry.
